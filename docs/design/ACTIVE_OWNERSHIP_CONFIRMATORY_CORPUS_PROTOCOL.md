## Purpose

The frozen 48-document review can decide whether the Item 4 classifier is worth carrying into a
return preregistration. It cannot establish the declared accuracy thresholds at one-sided 95%
confidence. This protocol defines the separate confirmation study required before sleeve
admission. It does not replace, soften, or reinterpret the 48-row point gate.

## Activation

This study activates only if the original 48-row packet passes its unchanged 95% precision, 80%
recall, and 90% exact-ownership point gates through the governed independent-review importer. If
that packet fails, this protocol is retired without acquiring confirmation documents, the labels
do not become a tuning set, and returns remain closed for the identity.

## Population and deterministic selection

The target population is initial Schedule 13D filings from 2010 through 2025 that have successful
SEC header lineage and exactly one contemporaneous ticker mapping. Every accession in the original
160-document feasibility corpus is excluded.

For each year, all remaining index accessions are ordered by SHA-256 of the fixed namespace
`alphac-active-ownership-confirmatory-v1`, year, and accession. Headers are fetched in deterministic
50-row increments until at least 40 eligible, disjoint rows exist for that year or the year is
exhausted. The first 40 eligible rows by the same rank are selected. Any year with fewer than 40
eligible rows makes the design data-gated; rows may not be borrowed from another year.

The resulting corpus contains exactly 640 rows: 40 from each of 16 years. Selection uses no
document text, classifier output, human label, price, return, or portfolio result. After the 640
accessions are frozen, the unchanged v3 document parser and active-intent classifier are run once.
The prediction-blind reviewer packet is then built from source text without machine outputs.

## Confirmation gates

The independent reviewer applies the same source-label definitions and attests that machine
outputs, prices, returns, and automated or AI labeling assistance were not consulted. The report
publishes raw confusion counts, point estimates, and one-sided 95% exact Clopper-Pearson lower
bounds.

Sleeve admission requires all of the following on the fixed 640-row corpus:

- precision point estimate at least 95% and its lower bound at least 95%;
- recall point estimate at least 80% and its lower bound at least 80%;
- exact ownership agreement at least 90% and its lower bound at least 90%;
- at least 59 machine-predicted positives and 14 human-positive rows, the minimum denominators at
  which perfect outcomes can reach the corresponding confidence bounds; and
- the unchanged machine extraction gates continue to pass on the disjoint corpus.

The 640-row size was fixed before outcomes. At the feasibility corpus's 20% machine-positive rate,
it would contain about 128 predicted positives. That planning estimate would allow at most two
false positives while retaining a 95% lower confidence bound. The estimate is not a promise: the
actual frozen denominator controls, and an underpowered or failed corpus blocks admission.

## Current acquisition boundary

The cached 800-header feasibility sample contains only 218 eligible rows after excluding the
original 160, with between 5 and 23 per year. It is therefore insufficient for this design and may
not be mislabeled as a confirmatory sample. Additional headers and all confirmation submissions
remain unacquired.

The machine-readable design receipt is generated by
`scripts/seal_active_ownership_confirmatory_design.py` and binds this protocol, the original
sample, the metadata audit, and the pre-label gate audit.

## Claim boundary

This protocol fixes a future classifier-validation design. It proves no classifier accuracy,
investment return, Sharpe ratio, drawdown, correlation, capacity, sleeve admission, human review,
or external validation.
