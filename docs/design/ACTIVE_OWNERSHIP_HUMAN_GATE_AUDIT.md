# Active Ownership human-label gate audit

**Author:** Arhan Canli  
**Stage:** prospective, before independent labels and before any return access

The frozen 48-document review remains a useful feasibility gate, but it is not large enough to
support every statistical interpretation a reader might attach to the words “95% precision.” This
audit fixes that claim boundary before the reviewer opens the packet. It does not lower a threshold,
change a row, expose a prediction to the reviewer, or inspect a return.

## What the frozen design can test

The packet contains three filings from each year from 2010 through 2025. Eight of the 48 rows are
machine-predicted positives, 40 are machine-predicted negatives, 45 have extracted Item 4 text, and
three are unresolved extractions. The reviewer cannot see those machine assignments.

At the declared point thresholds, the integer pass conditions are deliberately sharp:

- precision of at least 95% requires 8/8 predicted positives to be true positives; one false
  positive fails;
- recall of at least 80% can pass only if the reviewer identifies at most ten human-positive rows,
  because the frozen classifier predicts eight positives; and
- ownership agreement of at least 90% requires at least 44/48 exact outcomes, allowing at most four
  mismatches.

These conditions are operationally reachable and can reject the parser. They are therefore kept
unchanged for the decision to proceed, or not proceed, to a separately preregistered return trial.

## What 48 labels cannot establish

Point estimates and confidence-bound evidence are different claims. Even if all eight predicted
positives are correct, the one-sided 95% exact Clopper–Pearson lower bound on precision is about
68.8%, not 95%. Even a perfect 48/48 binary result has a lower bound of about 93.9%. With no errors,
the smallest denominators whose one-sided 95% exact lower bounds reach the existing thresholds are
59 predicted-positive cases for 95% precision, 14 human-positive cases for 80% recall, and 29 rows
for 90% ownership agreement. Any errors require larger denominators.

The current packet may therefore produce a frozen feasibility point result. It may not be described
as statistically establishing 95% classifier precision. If it passes, the report must publish raw
confusion counts, point estimates, and exact confidence bounds.

## Prospective decision

The 48-row gate remains the gate for return-preregistration eligibility. Before sleeve admission,
a disjoint confirmatory corpus must be frozen and independently labeled. Its size must be driven by
the relevant positive denominators, and the one-sided 95% exact lower bounds—not just point
estimates—must meet the declared precision, recall, and ownership thresholds.

If the frozen 48-row point gate fails, this classifier is data-gated. Its labels may not become a
tuning set, and returns may not be opened for this identity.

The machine-readable receipt is generated by
`scripts/audit_active_ownership_human_gate.py`. It binds the frozen packet, source audit, feasibility
result, and this protocol by SHA-256.

## Claim boundary

This audit evaluates gate design and discrete reachability. It uses no human outcomes, prices,
returns, Sharpe ratios, drawdowns, correlations, capacity estimates, or portfolio results. It proves
neither classification accuracy nor investment performance.
