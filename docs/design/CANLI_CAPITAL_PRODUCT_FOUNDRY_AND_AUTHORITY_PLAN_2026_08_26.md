# Canli Capital Product, Foundry and Authority Plan

**Date:** 2026-08-26

**Owner and principal investigator:** Arhan Canli

**Status:** Governing execution plan

**Scope:** Public product, ALPHAC evidence system, continuous research infrastructure, authority program and application proof

**Explicitly deferred:** arXiv submission work

## 1. The outcome

Canli Capital will become a public research and verification system for systematic trading. A visitor
should understand the product in less than ten seconds, inspect broker-linked paper execution without
trusting a marketing claim, and follow a result from its preregistered identity through testing,
rejection or admission, execution and signed publication.

The project is successful when it earns belief through inspectable evidence. Visual ambition,
performance objectives, research volume and publicity remain subordinate to that rule.

The defining product sentence is:

> A new kind of quant lab: the strategy, the rejected trials, the broker state and the rules for
> believing the result live in one public record.

The defining technical shift is:

> From publishing returns to publishing the evidence required to believe them.

## 2. Honest category position

The surrounding category is not empty. Daru publishes reproducible quant research, Thales exposes
public paper strategies and rejected work, Sigmatic addresses institutional research governance,
and Papers With Backtest connects papers, datasets and executable research. Canli Capital should
not claim to be the first system of any broad kind unless a documented landscape review supports
the exact wording.

The defensible Canli Capital wedge is the joined system:

1. Hypothesis identity fixed before outcome inspection.
2. Complete union accounting for admitted, killed, blocked and incomplete trials.
3. Point-in-time data lineage and bounded research budgets.
4. Mutation-tested publication gates and deterministic replay.
5. Broker reconciliation for the forward paper record.
6. Signed, append-only public evidence with visible correction history.
7. Explicit maturity labels for observed, simulated and planned claims.

Public language may call the project revolutionary only when it describes this change in method,
not as an unsupported statement of market leadership. Preferred language is "a new evidence model
for quantitative research" or "prospective quant evidence infrastructure."

## 4. Product architecture

Canli Capital is the public institution. ALPHAC is the systematic portfolio and evidence protocol.
Foundry is the continuous research system. The live record is the observable output.

```text
Canli Capital
├── ALPHAC: portfolio, sleeves, risk and broker-linked paper execution
├── Foundry: preregistered testing, falsification, replay and candidate production
├── Evidence record: claims, artifacts, signatures, corrections and machine interfaces
└── Public product: live dashboard, research library, methodology and verification
```

This naming avoids presenting a website, a portfolio and a research cluster as the same thing.

and signed public claim. The object must be legible without reading decorative labels.

2. Pin a stable wrapper and animate children through transforms and opacity.
3. Lazy-load Three.js after meaningful first paint.
4. Pause rendering when offscreen or when the document is hidden.
5. Adapt pixel ratio, geometry and effects to device capability.
6. Use a static poster or lightweight 2D sequence on small and low-power devices.
7. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, show the full story without pinning or scrubbed motion.
8. Clean up every timeline, listener, renderer and animation frame on route changes.

Each loop includes an artifact timestamp, uses WebM and H.264 fallbacks, has a poster image and loads

## 8. Writing and claim system

The voice is young, precise and technically serious. It should sound like a capable builder who
knows what the evidence cannot yet prove. It must not imitate institutional jargon or manufacture
seniority.

Writing rules:

1. No em dash character in rendered user-facing copy, metadata or structured data.
2. No unsupported `first`, `best`, `revolutionary`, `institutional-grade` or `proven` claim.
3. Prefer concrete nouns, short sentences and active verbs.
4. Label every performance claim as observed, simulated, model-estimated or planned.
5. Put the limitation next to the claim, not in a remote disclaimer.
6. Use Arhan Canli's name where authorship is material, never as decorative repetition.
7. Preserve immutable raw artifacts. Presentation normalization must never silently change a hash.
8. Require a build check for em dashes across rendered indexable HTML, metadata and JSON-LD.

The current corpus contains legacy em dashes. Migration will distinguish editable presentation copy
from hash-bound source artifacts. Historical artifacts remain untouched and are linked as originals.

## 9. Foundry: the continuous research engine

Foundry runs continuously on DigitalOcean, but it does not generate and test unlimited hypotheses.
It performs approved research, data QA, deterministic replay, falsification, stress testing and
publication work inside a bounded identity budget.

### Isolation boundary

The research cluster and the live execution node must be separate.

```text
Research network                         Execution network
researchd -> workers -> validator        ALPHAC live node -> Alpaca paper accounts
     |           |          |                    |
 Postgres    DO Spaces   holdout vault       broker reconciler
     |
sanitizer -> public read-only artifacts -> Canli Capital
```

1. The live node alone holds broker write credentials.
2. Research workers have no broker write keys.
3. The holdout vault uses separate credentials and network scope.
4. The publisher receives sanitized artifacts only.
5. Separate deployment principals prevent a website compromise from reaching execution.

### Components

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `researchd` | Queue policy, reservations, leases, quotas and run lifecycle |
| Postgres | Authoritative identity registry, queue, metadata and audit events |
| DigitalOcean Spaces | Immutable data snapshots, logs, packets and replay artifacts |
| Rootless workers | Isolated data preparation, tests, stress jobs and report builds |
| Data gateway | Allowlisted, point-in-time source access with lineage capture |
| Validator | Cost checks, DSR, PBO, CPCV, stress tests and deterministic reruns |
| Holdout vault | One-shot sealed evaluation under separate authorization |
| Sanitizer | Secret scanning, field allowlists and public artifact construction |
| Sentinel | Health, drift, resource, queue and integrity alerts |
| Status adapter | Read-only public metrics with no sensitive infrastructure details |

Postgres can initially provide worker leases with `SKIP LOCKED`, avoiding a second queue dependency.
Redis becomes justified only if measured contention or scheduling latency requires it.

### Research lifecycle

1. Run metadata-only feasibility checks without spending a hypothesis identity.
2. Require human approval and a preregistered hypothesis before outcome-bearing work.
3. Reserve and hash the trial identity.
4. Freeze the point-in-time data snapshot and transformation lineage.
5. Run a bounded test matrix declared in advance.
6. Apply costs, stress, deflated Sharpe, PBO and CPCV where appropriate.
7. Reproduce the result in a clean worker.
8. Authorize a one-shot holdout only through the separate holdout policy.
9. Admit, kill or data-gate the identity.
10. If admitted, place the candidate in paper shadow before sleeve consideration.
11. Publish the complete packet, including negative outcomes and limitations.

### Security and reliability contract

1. Rootless containers with `no-new-privileges`, seccomp profiles and read-only base images.
2. CPU, memory, disk, network and wall-time quotas per job.
3. Outbound network allowlists and no arbitrary code execution on the host.
4. Secrets outside Git with least privilege and regular rotation.
5. Immutable audit events for every state transition.
6. Signed builds and pinned images for production workers.
7. Daily recovery checks and scheduled restore drills.
8. Alerts for queue stalls, stale data, failed replay, quota breach and publication mismatch.

### Public Foundry status

The public page may show compute hours, queue depth, identities spent, admitted, killed and data-gated
counts, latest run hash and replay status. It must not expose credentials, provider internals, source
licensing restrictions, unpublished hypotheses or holdout contents.

## 10. Sleeve and portfolio program

The objective remains a forward Sharpe of 1.50 with controlled drawdown and genuinely diversifying
sleeves. Foundry exists to improve the quality of attempts, not to guarantee the result.

The admission scorecard for a candidate sleeve includes:

1. Economic mechanism stated before result inspection.
2. Point-in-time data and reproducible lineage.
3. Net performance after realistic costs and latency.
4. Robustness across regimes, perturbations and relevant cross-validation.
5. Deflated significance under the complete trial union.
6. Incremental portfolio value, not standalone Sharpe alone.
7. Correlation confidence bounds and stressed correlation.
8. Capacity, turnover, slippage and failure-mode analysis.
9. Clean deterministic rerun.
10. Paper-shadow evidence before portfolio admission.

The system may finish with fewer than fourteen sleeves. That is a credible result if the record shows
that weaker candidates were rejected under rules fixed in advance.

Technical SEO is currently strong enough that authority, citations and uniquely useful public tools

than purchased or exchanged.

1. Publish one canonical research object on Canli Capital.

3. Create a concise technical note that points to the canonical object.
4. Send targeted, personal outreach to researchers working on the exact method or dataset.

### SEO acceptance gates

1. Zero broken internal links and zero orphaned indexable pages.
2. Unique descriptive title, description and one H1 per indexable page.
3. Correct canonical, robots directive, Open Graph image and structured data.
4. Complete breadcrumbs and contextual related-work links.
5. Valid sitemap partitions and feeds.
6. Search Console ownership and monitoring completed by the account owner.
7. Monthly review of relevant referring domains, citations, impressions and qualified queries.

## 16. Non-goals and authorization boundaries

1. No real-money deployment is implied by paper-funded broker accounts.
2. No performance target is guaranteed.
3. No gate is lowered merely to make a result pass.
4. No purchased backlinks, fake reviews, fabricated traffic or false institutional affiliation.
5. No silent rewrite of immutable research artifacts.
6. No secret, broker key or private hypothesis in a public build.
7. No automatic execution of unreviewed model-generated strategy code.
8. No production infrastructure mutation without a reviewed deployment and rollback path.
9. No publication claim whose support cannot be identified by artifact and version.
