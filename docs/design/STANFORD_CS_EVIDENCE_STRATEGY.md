# Stanford CS application evidence strategy for ALPHAC

**Applicant and project author:** Arhan Canli  
**Status:** truthful application-planning document, not an admissions claim

## The governing constraint

Stanford Undergraduate Admission states that it does not add research papers or other special work
to an application file and does not guarantee that application readers will open links. ALPHAC
therefore cannot depend on an admissions officer reading a 30-page paper or exploring a dashboard.
The application itself must communicate the intellectual question, Arhan's decisions, measurable
impact, failures and growth in very little space.

- [Stanford: after submitting an application](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/submitted.html)
- [Stanford: holistic admission](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/overview/index.html)
- [Stanford: application and essays](https://admission.stanford.edu/apply/first-year/apply.html)

Stanford also says applicants apply to the university as a whole, not directly to the CS major.
The project should demonstrate intellectual vitality and contribution without pretending that
technical spectacle substitutes for academic strength, character or the rest of the application.

## What makes ALPHAC compelling

The strongest story is not “I built an algorithm with a high Sharpe.” It is:

1. Arhan asked whether quantitative-investment claims could be made independently verifiable.
2. He built a point-in-time research, execution, governance and publication system spanning data
   engineering, statistics, distributed operations, web systems and human-readable evidence.
3. The system repeatedly contradicted attractive claims, and he published the corrections rather
   than hiding them.
4. He redesigned impossible gates using statistical power and decision scope without regrading
   known candidates.
5. He put the surviving ideas into broker-separated paper accounts and made the provenance public.
6. He created durable tools and research objects other people can inspect and reproduce.

This shows computer-science depth through systems design, correctness, testing, provenance,
security, interfaces and reproducibility. Finance is the demanding application domain, not the
entire identity of the applicant.

## Evidence that must exist before the application

- a one-page public project map understandable in under 90 seconds;
- a concise founder contribution statement distinguishing Arhan's work from tools and references;
- one flagship methodological paper and four advanced sleeve papers;
- at least one external DOI-backed reproducibility bundle;
- at least one substantive external review, replication attempt or technical critique;
- a compact table of important failures and what changed because of each one;
- a continuous broker-reconciled paper record with its limitations visible;
- a short public engineering demo showing one claim traced from website to broker/artifact/code;
- credible usage or contribution evidence if anyone outside the project engages with it; and
- an application-ready 150-character activity description plus longer factual evidence memo.

No item may be manufactured merely for optics. External review means a real independent reviewer;
usage means actual usage; publication means a record that exists and can be resolved.

## Application narrative boundary

Do not call repository posting peer review, paper trading live money, a simulation a return record,
or a target an achievement. Do not imply Stanford endorsed the project. Do not overload essays with
metrics. The memorable material is Arhan's curiosity, judgment and willingness to make a system
that can prove him wrong.

The public project may support a brief application reference, but the application must remain
complete if no reader follows that reference. A teacher or additional recommender should discuss
Arhan's intellectual process only from direct personal knowledge, not from a script written for
them.
